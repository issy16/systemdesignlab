      What & when
      Metrics

      Aggregated numbers: request rate, error rate, latency percentiles, CPU. Cheap, fast to query, perfect for dashboards and alerts.

      Why
      Logs

      One line per event, with details. Structured logs (JSON with fields) can be searched and filtered.

      Where
      Traces

      The path of one request across services, with the time spent in each. Shows exactly which hop is slow.

      The one difference

      Monitoring checks for problems you predicted: dashboards and alerts on known metrics. Observability is being able to answer questions you didn't predict, like “why is checkout slow for some fans since 14:05?”, by combining metrics, logs and traces that share the same ids and labels.

      Notice
      Metrics + alerts
      Graph latency, errors and traffic for every endpoint. Alert on what users feel (P99, error rate), not on CPU.
      
    
      Explain
      Logs
      Structured, searchable, with a request or trace id on every line so you can jump from a log to its trace.
      
    
      Pinpoint
      Traces
      Propagate a trace id through every service call. The waterfall shows where time goes.
      
  
      
  
      The tradeoff
      Telemetry costs money and attention: storing every log and trace is expensive, and noisy alerts teach people to ignore them. Keep metrics for everything, sample traces, keep logs structured, and alert only on symptoms someone must act on.
