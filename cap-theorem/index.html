    Consistency first (CP)

    If a side can't confirm with the official count, it refuses. Every answer is correct, but some fans get an error until the network heals.

    Availability first (AP)

    Every side keeps answering with what it knows. Nobody gets an error, but the two sides can disagree, and you have to clean up the conflicts afterwards.

    The one difference

    The CAP theorem says that when the network is partitioned (split so parts can't talk), a system must choose: stay consistent by refusing some requests, or stay available by answering with data that might be wrong. Partitions aren't optional, so the real question is which one you pick, for each kind of data.

    It will happen
    Network partition
    Links fail, routers misbehave, a data center goes dark. Parts of your system can't talk while still running.
    
    
    The choice
    CAP
    During a partition: refuse to stay correct (CP), or answer to stay up (AP). You can't have both at once.
    
    
    Per data
    Choose deliberately
    Seats and payments: consistency. Likes, view counts, recommendations: availability. One app can make different choices for each.
    
  
    
  
    The tradeoff
    CP costs availability during partitions; AP costs correctness and needs conflict resolution afterwards. Even without a partition there's a smaller version of the same trade: waiting for the other side to confirm (consistent) is slower than answering locally (fast).
