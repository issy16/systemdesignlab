    Eventual consistency

    Replicas are always a little behind. If writes stop, they catch up and agree with the primary eventually. Until then, a read can return old data. That's fine for a concert list, not for “did my purchase go through?”.

    Failover

    When the primary dies, promote a replica to be the new primary. Done by a person, it takes minutes and writes fail meanwhile. Done automatically, it takes seconds, but any writes the replica hadn't received yet are gone.

    The one difference

    With async replication the primary confirms a write right away and sends it to the replicas later: fast, but if the primary dies, its last moments of writes die with it. With sync replication the primary waits until a replica has the write: every write is slower, but a failover loses nothing.

    Copies
    Replication
    The primary streams every change to replicas. Gives you spare copies for failover, and extra servers for reads.
    
    
    Behind
    Read replicas + lag
    Reads from replicas take load off the primary but may be stale. Send “read your own write” queries to the primary.
    
    
    Take over
    Failover
    Promote a replica when the primary dies. Automatic is fast. Async replication can lose the last writes; sync can't.
    
  
    
  
    The tradeoff
    Replicas buy read capacity and survival at the cost of consistency. Async: fast writes, stale reads, a small chance of losing recent writes. Sync: slower writes and no loss. Many teams use one sync replica (no loss) plus async ones for reads.
