Message queue in one line

Instead of doing slow work while someone waits, the app puts a small note (a message) on a queue and answers right away. Separate workers take messages off the queue and do the work at their own pace.

    Backpressure

    When the consumer can't keep up, the system tells the producer to slow down (refuse, wait or retry later) instead of buffering forever. A queue with no limit just turns “slow” into “out of memory”.

    Dead letter queue

    A message that fails again and again (a poison message) is moved aside after a few tries, so it stops blocking workers. A person or a tool looks at the dead letter queue later.

    The one difference

    Without a queue, the fan waits for the slowest step. With a queue, the fan waits only for the steps that matter right now, and the rest catch up. The queue absorbs spikes, but only if its size is limited and its failures go somewhere.

    Decouple
    Message queue
    Producers drop messages, workers process them later. Fast responses, and spikes get absorbed instead of passed on.
    
    
    Slow down
    Backpressure
    Limit the queue and push back on producers when it's full. Bounded is better than out of memory.
    
    
    Set aside
    Dead letter queue
    After N failed tries, move the message aside so one bad message can't keep a worker busy forever.
    
  
    
  
    The tradeoff
    A queue makes work eventually happen instead of happening now. That's perfect for emails, PDFs and analytics, but the fan's seat must still be reserved during checkout. Watch the queue's length and age: they're your early warning.
