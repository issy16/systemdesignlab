    Backups and RPO

    RPO (recovery point objective) is how much data you can afford to lose: the time between the last good copy and the disaster. Nightly backups mean up to 24 h of loss. Continuous archiving of the database log allows recovery to any second.

    Disaster recovery and RTO

    RTO (recovery time objective) is how long getting back can take. A backup you've never restored has an unknown RTO, and sometimes it doesn't work at all. Practice restores.

    The one difference

    A blameful postmortem asks “who messed up?”, and people learn to hide mistakes. A blameless postmortem asks “what let a normal mistake reach production, and what will catch it next time?”, and the system actually gets safer.

    Postmortem
    Blameless: impact, timeline, root cause in the system, what went well, and action items with owners and dates.
    
  
    
  
    The tradeoff
    Better RPO and RTO cost money: log archiving, standby databases, practice drills. Decide what each kind of data is worth losing, and test that you can actually meet it. Every page in this course reduces how often incidents happen; this one is about how much they hurt when they do.
